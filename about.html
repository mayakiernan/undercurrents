A research project exploring the intersection of climate adaptation and insurance, conducted in Fall 2026 as part of the Harvard Master in Design Engineering final-year studio.

Inspired by growing warnings from reinsurers of a looming global catastrophe unless governments and private entities take climate risks and adaptation more seriously, this project examines how insurance can help drive adaptation. From the cost-saving potential of hail nets and how to better design their deployment, to the economics of calculating long-term savings from adaptation measures, the project explores the role of data, economic projections, and insurance in understanding and incentivizing climate resilience.
